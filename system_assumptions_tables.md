**1.2 System Assumptions**

<< Document any assumptions you made about how the system will operate. You must explicitly state and justify the assumptions used for timing, traffic coordination, and congestion control. You should also state any assumptions regarding differences in traffic demand and priority between roads R1–R5. >>

To bridge the gap between abstract requirements and concrete software implementation, specific operational parameters must be established. The following tables outline the foundational parameters guiding the system architecture, ensuring realistic traffic flow, correct safety behaviour, and reliable interprocess communication.

**Revision note:** this table replaces an earlier draft. Several earlier assumptions (a 60 s master cycle, per-road vehicle-per-hour demand figures, meter-precise inter-segment distances, exclusion of left turns, and a diagonal multi-crossing train-propagation model) are **no longer part of the design** and have been superseded by the values below, which are derived directly from the finalised project specification. Where the finalised design deliberately does _not_ commit to a numeric value (e.g. exact traffic volumes), this is stated explicitly rather than an invented number being carried forward.

**Formatting convention:** an assumption marked with **\*** has a supporting numeric derivation shown in a short calculation table immediately below its section, rather than inline in the table cell, to keep the assumption tables themselves concise. External sources are cited in IEEE numbered style `[n]`, with the full reference list at the end of this document.

_Note on NU-02 — why a permissive left turn is not treated as an unresolved signal conflict:_ a vehicle conflict at a signalised intersection falls into one of two categories. The first is a conflict with **no defined right-of-way rule** — for example, two different, uncoordinated roads both showing green at once. No driver behaviour can resolve that safely; it must be prevented by the signal logic itself, and the design's intersection conflict matrix exists specifically to prevent it. The second is a conflict that **already has a defined right-of-way rule** — a through movement and the opposing direction's permissive left turn, where the turning driver is required to yield to oncoming traffic. This is the standard operating pattern at the majority of signalised intersections worldwide; the residual risk depends on driver judgement, not on an ambiguity in the signal state. NU-02 accepts this second, already-resolved category rather than eliminating it with a dedicated-lane, protected-turn design (NU-03), on the basis of the cycle-length, complexity, and schedule trade-offs described above.

| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| RC-01   | Each crossing (RC1–RC3) has exactly **two boom-gate arms, two road-facing flashing-light sets, two train signals** (one per rail direction), **two train-approach sensors**, **two boom-gate position sensors**, and **two exit/clearance sensors** (present in the physical inventory but not wired into the gate-reopening decision — see RC-05).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Symmetric two-way protection consistent with the double-track, bidirectional railway corridor described in the brief.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | `RLx` owns and exclusively actuates all of the above; no other controller, including `C1`, may issue an actuation command to any of this equipment, under any circumstance.                                                                                                      |
| RC-02   | Each crossing is managed by its own dedicated railway local controller (`RL1–RL3`), architecturally and logically distinct from the six road-intersection local controllers (`L1–L6`); road controllers may only **read** crossing status, never command it. **`RL1`, `RL2`, and `RL3` operate completely independently of one another** — they share no state and there is no cross-crossing coordination or propagation model (e.g. no assumption that a single train sequentially triggers all three crossings). This is a distinct concept from `TC-xx` (Traffic Coordination), which coordinates _road_ intersections along an arterial (`L1-L3-L5` and `L2-L4-L6`) — the two are unrelated. | Directly required by the lecturer's clarification that intersection controllers may only sense railway status, and that only the railway-crossing controller may actuate boom gates, flashers, and the train signal. The three crossings are physically separate locations on the network (NU-06) with no shared equipment or shared detection, so there is no basis for coordinating them with each other.                                                                                                             | The status link from `RLx` to the two adjacent `Lx` controllers is strictly one-directional and read-only; no message or shared state ever passes between `RL1`, `RL2`, and `RL3`.                                                                                               |
| RC-03\* | The warning-to-arrival lead time (train-approach-sensor trip → train physically at the crossing) is **derived, not assumed outright**, from four components (flashing warning, gate closing, confirmation margin, adjacent-intersection clearance margin) to a target of **approximately 45 s**. Approach-sensor placement distance follows from this lead time and the assumed maximum train speed (RC-08).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | The brief provides no lead-time figure. A methodology-derived value is safer and more defensible than an arbitrary one, and 45 s (rather than a shorter figure) explicitly accounts for the _adjacent road intersection's_ own clearance sequence, not the gate alone.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Sets the real-time deadline budget for the entire railway safety chain and the physical sensor placement distance; both values are explicitly tunable, not fixed facts. See the calculation table below for the four components and their sum.                                   |
| RC-04   | Every simulated train is assumed to take the same fixed, known amount of time (**approximately 20 s**) to fully clear the crossing once it arrives — a "crossing-occupancy window" computed as `expected arrival time + fixed clearance duration`. The gate does not reopen while any train's occupancy window (either direction, including a second train detected before the first clears) has not yet elapsed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Deliberately replaces reliance on a real-time exit-sensor confirmation with a simpler, uniform timing assumption, adopted specifically to simplify the Core reopening decision to a timer rather than requiring live exit detection.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Gate-reopening logic in `RLx` is timer-based, not exit-sensor-based, in the Core design; this correctly handles a second train detected while the first is still occupying the crossing without special-case logic, by adding its window to the set that must all elapse.        |
| RC-05   | **Accepted trade-off:** because reopening uses a fixed-duration timer rather than a confirmed-exit signal, the Core design does not detect the specific edge case of an abnormally slow or stalled train still on the crossing after its assumed occupancy window has elapsed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | A deliberate, explicitly documented simplification for the Core scope — not an oversight. Building live exit confirmation into the Core safety decision was judged to add more implementation risk than safety value within the project's fixed timeframe.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | The physical exit/clearance sensor (RC-01) is retained as the identified path to close this gap in a future enhancement (e.g. cross-checking actual clearance against the assumed window to flag an anomalous train), but it is not wired into any Core decision today.          |
| RC-06   | A train signal never shows `PROCEED` unless the boom gate is **sensor-confirmed** `CLOSED` at the moment of the decision. The fixed 10 s gate-closing duration used in RC-03's lead-time budget is a scheduling estimate only and is **never** substituted for an actual sensor confirmation; if confirmation has not arrived by the required deadline, the train signal defaults to `STOP` and a fault is raised.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Keeps the "planning layer" (assumed durations, used only to schedule warning/clearance timing in advance) strictly separate from the "verification layer" (the real sensor reading, which alone gates the safety-critical `PROCEED` decision) — so a delayed physical gate can never be silently treated as "probably closed by now."                                                                                                                                                                                   | This is treated as an absolute rule with no exception: ambiguity always resolves toward stopping the train, never toward assuming it is safe to proceed; the boom-gate position sensor (RC-01) is the sole authority for the `PROCEED` decision, never a timer.                  |
| RC-07   | All three `RL1`, `RL2`, `RL3` instances run **identical** control logic; no crossing-specific behavioural differences are modelled.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | The brief gives no reason for the three crossings to differ; inventing per-crossing differences would only increase the testing surface without teaching or assessment value.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | A single generic railway-controller implementation is deployed three times, differing only in configuration (e.g. device/peer addresses), not in logic.                                                                                                                          |
| RC-08   | Assumed **maximum train speed = 80 km/h (22.2 m/s)**, used consistently wherever a train velocity is needed.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | No train speed is given in the brief; 80 km/h is a representative value for a regional/commuter double-track line, stated explicitly as its own assumption (rather than left implicit inside a derived figure) so it can be reviewed and tuned independently.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Feeds the approach-sensor placement-distance calculation (RC-03) below; if this value changes, only the placement distance is recalculated — the 45 s lead-time budget itself does not depend on it.                                                                             |
| RC-09   | `C1` may send a **high-level, validated command** to a railway-crossing local controller `RLx` (e.g. acknowledging/clearing a reported fault once physical repair is confirmed) — consistent with the brief's statement that operators "will occasionally send commands to the intersections, boom gate control and train approach signals system." The command is always addressed to `RLx`, which independently validates and decides whether to act; `C1` never issues a raw actuation instruction (e.g. "set gate down," "set train green") directly to a boom gate, flasher, or train signal.                                                                                                | Reconciles two brief statements that could otherwise look contradictory: operators may send commands "to... boom gate control and train approach signals system," while the same brief and the lecturer's clarification are explicit that only the railway-crossing controller may actuate this equipment, and intersection controllers may only sense it. The resolution is the same command-abstraction principle already used for `Lx` (PA-10): Central commands the owning controller, never the hardware directly. | `RLx`'s command handler validates any `C1` request exactly as `Lx` does (PA-10) — rejecting/NACKing anything unsafe (e.g. a fault-clear request while the underlying fault condition still physically exists). No new actuation path to railway hardware is introduced for `C1`. |
| RC-10 | A railway-equipment fault (e.g. a boom gate that fails to confirm closed, or a stuck-open gate with a train approaching) is handled by `RLx` **entirely locally and immediately**: forcing the train signal to `STOP` and reporting the fault to `C1` are two **independent, parallel** actions — `RLx` never waits for a `C1` acknowledgement before forcing `STOP`. | The lecturer's in-class clarification describes the train receiving a red/stop signal as soon as a boom-gate issue exists, with the error *then* reported to the control room — not a round trip through Central before the train signal changes. Making the safety action depend on a remote acknowledgement would directly contradict the local-autonomy principle (PA-08/PA-11) for the single most safety-critical decision in the system. | `RLx`'s fault-handling logic never blocks on, or waits for, any reply from `C1` before forcing `STOP`; the fault report to `C1` is a fire-and-forget status message, not a request awaiting permission. |
| RC-11 | The train-approach sensor's failure detection is scoped to exactly one case: a sensor **stuck active** — signalling `TRAIN_APPROACHING` continuously well beyond the derived lead-time-plus-occupancy budget (RC-03/RC-04) with no train ever confirmed clear — which `RLx` treats as a fault and fails toward the safe direction (`WARNING`/gates down). A sensor that goes **permanently silent** is explicitly **not** claimed to be detectable: from `RLx`'s perspective it is indistinguishable from "no train is currently approaching," since the Core sensor design has no independent heartbeat/self-test signal separate from its detection line. | A single binary detection line cannot report its own health — it can only report "train" or "no train." Claiming the system detects a silently dead sensor would overstate what this sensor type can do; scoping the claim to the one case that genuinely is detectable (a signal that never clears) keeps the safety argument honest and defensible under direct questioning. | A permanently dead approach sensor silently removes that crossing's proactive warning capability — the **fail-open** direction, not fail-safe. This is an explicitly accepted residual risk in the Core design, not a claimed capability. The identified mitigation — a periodic self-test/heartbeat signal on the sensor, independent of its detection line — is out of Core scope and would be the natural next hardening step if the design were extended beyond this course. |

_Lead-time calculation (supports RC-03):_

| Component                                                  | Duration | Running total |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------------- |
| Flashing-only warning before gate starts closing           | 5 s      | 5 s           |
| Gate-closing duration                                      | 10 s     | 15 s          |
| Closed-confirmation safety margin                          | 5 s      | 20 s          |
| Adjacent-intersection's own safe clearance-sequence margin | 25 s     | **45 s**      |

_Approach-sensor placement distance = lead time × train speed (RC-08) = 45 s × 22.2 m/s ≈ 1000 m._

**1.2.7 Pedestrian, Sensing, and Abnormal-Operation Assumptions (PA-xx)**

_Pedestrian Operation_

| **ID** | **Assumption**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | **Justification**                                                                                                                                                                                | **Design Implication**                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PA-01  | Pedestrian crossings exist **only at the six road intersections**; no pedestrian crossing is modelled at the railway crossings themselves.                                                                                                                                         | The brief only places pedestrian crossings "at each intersection"; this also simplifies the pedestrian/pre-emption interaction analysis, since a pedestrian is never physically near the tracks. | Railway pre-emption (CC-02) never needs to interrupt a pedestrian phase to protect the crossing — the two concerns are architecturally independent.                         |
| PA-02  | A pedestrian request arriving while railway pre-emption is active at that intersection is **latched, not discarded**, and is normally still served in the intersection's ordinary non-conflicting phase, since pre-emption only forces the toward-crossing _vehicle_ movement red. | Consistent with PA-01 — since pedestrian crossings are never on the path pre-emption must protect, there is no reason to ever drop a pedestrian request due to a railway event.                  | The pedestrian request-latch mechanism has no special-cased "railway override" branch; it behaves identically regardless of concurrent railway state.                       |
| PA-03  | A pedestrian push-button that is stuck (never releases) or becomes unresponsive is treated, fail-safe, as a **standing pending request** (assume real demand exists) rather than ignored, and the condition is reported to `C1` as a fault.                                        | Treating an ambiguous/failed button as "no demand" could permanently deny pedestrian service at that crossing; assuming demand is the fail-safe direction.                                       | A "stuck button" fault does not block pedestrian service — it guarantees it (at the cost of always scheduling that crossing's WALK) while separately alerting the operator. |

_Vehicle and Railway Sensing_

| **ID** | **Assumption**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | **Justification**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | **Design Implication**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| PA-04  | One logical vehicle-presence sensor is used **per approach** (not per lane), reporting **binary presence/absence only** — never a vehicle count or measured queue length.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Approach-level, presence-only sensing is sufficient to drive `OFF_PEAK_SENSOR` phase selection and is realistic (equivalent to a standard inductive loop), without requiring vehicle-counting hardware or logic the assessed design does not need.                                                                                              | Four logical vehicle-presence inputs exist per intersection; sensor logic is a simple boolean trigger for phase selection/extension.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| PA-05  | The **ordinary stop-line vehicle-presence sensor** (one per approach, PA-04) is monitored continuously in both modes for **status/fault purposes only** (PA-07) — it never drives phase selection or extension while `PEAK_FIXED` is active, only in `OFF_PEAK_SENSOR`. Congestion warning is an entirely separate concern, provided only by the dedicated advance/queue sensor defined in CC-01 (a different sensor, at a different location, only on the two railway-facing approaches) — the two must not be conflated. | Allowing the ordinary presence sensor to alter timing during `PEAK_FIXED` would undermine the fixed, predictable cycle length that green-wave coordination requires; separately, congestion detection specifically near a railway crossing needs the advance/queue sensor's distinct placement and semantics, not the ordinary approach sensor. | Two functionally distinct sensor types exist: the ordinary presence sensor (PA-04) feeds phase-extension logic only in `OFF_PEAK_SENSOR` and fault detection in both modes; the advance/queue sensor (CC-01) feeds only the congestion-suppression/drain logic (CC-02/CC-03), independent of which traffic mode is active. |
| PA-06  | Pedestrian demand relies strictly on push-button actuation; **no passive/automatic pedestrian-presence detection** is modelled.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Push-buttons provide a deterministic, directly demonstrable input, consistent with the brief's keyboard-simulated sensor model.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | The local controller never infers pedestrian demand automatically — a `PED_REQUEST` event only originates from a simulated button press.                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| PA-07  | A single vehicle-presence-sensor failure is declared only after an unrealistically long silence window relative to the expected cycle time; the affected approach then fails safe to **"assume demand present"** (so it can never be starved), without affecting any other approach at the same intersection.                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Treating a dead sensor as "no vehicles" would permanently deny service to that approach — a real safety/fairness concern, not just a technical inconvenience.                                                                                                                                                                                   | Sensor-failure fallback is per-approach and local; it never escalates to a full-intersection fixed-timing fallback for a single failed sensor.                                                                                                                                                                             |

_Abnormal / Failure Operation_

| **ID** | **Assumption**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | **Justification**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | **Design Implication**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| PA-08  | Upon losing communication with `C1` (heartbeat timeout — 1 s heartbeat period, link declared down after 3 consecutive missed heartbeats), a local controller **retains its last known valid _timing parameters_** (green/yellow/all-red durations, extension caps, coordination offset) rather than reverting to an arbitrary hard-coded default. **Mode selection itself (Peak vs. Off-Peak) is not frozen** — from the moment of disconnection onward, the local controller's own time-of-day clock becomes the sole authority for `PEAK_FIXED` ↔ `OFF_PEAK_SENSOR` switching, exactly as it would if `C1` were reachable. (Earlier wording said the controller keeps its "last known mode," which conflicts with the local-clock mode-switching rule elsewhere in the design; this row corrects that.) | Directly required by the brief ("must continue to operate if the communication link fails or the central controller goes offline"). Freezing the _timing parameters_ avoids an unpredictable behaviour change purely because of a comms outage; but freezing the _mode_ as well would mean a controller that disconnects during Peak stays in `PEAK_FIXED` forever, even hours later during genuine Off-Peak conditions — clearly not the intended fail-safe behaviour. | A disconnected local controller can still transition `PEAK_FIXED → OFF_PEAK_SENSOR` (or back) on its own clock while `C1` is unreachable, using the last validated timing profile for whichever mode is currently selected; `DEGRADED_LOCAL` is a continuation of known-good _configuration_, not a frozen _mode_. |
| PA-09  | On reconnection with `C1`, the local controller **pushes its full current actual state up to `C1` first**; `C1` does not push commands down onto a reconnecting controller until that state has been received.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Avoids a window where `C1` could impose a stale command on top of local state that has already moved on during the outage — `C1`'s view, not the local controller's, is what is stale.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | A "local push, then Central resumes commanding" handshake is required on every reconnection event, not a "Central re-asserts its last command" pattern.                                                                                                                                                            |
| PA-10  | A `C1` command that would violate any safety invariant is **rejected and reported (NACK)** by the receiving local controller, never silently applied — including, for example, a `REQUEST_OVERRIDE` that would conflict with active railway pre-emption, or one that would truncate an in-progress pedestrian clearance.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Required by the architectural principle that Central authority is monitoring, coordination, and _bounded_ override — never authority to force an unsafe state. Malformed or unsafe commands must not be able to force the intersection into an undefined state.                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Every command handler in the local controller validates against the safety invariants before acting, and always returns an explicit ACK/NACK rather than silently dropping or silently complying.                                                                                                                  |
| PA-11  | A single local controller's own software/hardware fault (or, for `RLx`, an unconfirmed gate state under train risk) causes that controller to enter `FAULT_SAFE` and force its actuators to their documented safe states (e.g. all-way flashing red at a road intersection; boom gate held `DOWN` and train signal held `STOP` at a railway crossing) — **never** to freeze or otherwise affect the rest of the network.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | A single point of failure at one intersection or crossing must not cascade into an unsafe or undefined state anywhere else in the network.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | `FAULT_SAFE` is local and self-contained; realised in the proof-of-concept via a lightweight per-node QNX watchdog/supervisor process that detects the primary controller process's death and forces the safe-state outputs directly.                                                                              |
| PA-12  | A `C1` (Central Controller) failure or unreachability does **not** force any intersection into an all-red or otherwise degraded output state; every local controller continues autonomous cycling exactly as if Central were present, only losing network-wide coordination and override capability.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Directly required by the brief; a single monitoring/coordination point must never be a safety-relevant single point of failure for the road network.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | The system degrades gracefully — coordination/override availability may be lost, but local intersection and railway-crossing safety behaviour is entirely preserved.                                                                                                                                               |
